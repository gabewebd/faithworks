A goal, a few steps, the right tools, and you at the end

1. Goal

You describe the outcome and the limits.

2. Plan

It breaks the goal into steps.

3. Act

It uses a tool you approved.

4. Check

It reads the result and picks the next step.

Repeats until the task is done, or it gets stuck

5. You review

Nothing important is sent, paid or deleted without your yes.

The billboard test

Before you paste, picture it on a billboard

Would I be fine seeing this on a billboard by the highway?

Your name, a client’s details, your password, your company’s plans, in letters three metres high.

If yes

Paste it

Public, general or made-up information.

If you’re not sure

Redact it first

Swap names and numbers for placeholders, or use a tool your work has approved.

If no

Keep it out

Passwords, codes, card numbers, IDs, other people’s private details.

A habit, not a prediction: most prompts never become public. The test protects you on the day one does.

What to share, and where

Sort information into three groups before you paste

Never paste

  • Passwords and PINs
  • One-time and recovery codes
  • API keys and access tokens
  • Card and bank account numbers
  • Passport, SSS, TIN and other ID numbers
  • Someone else’s health or legal details

Approved tools only

  • Customer and employee records
  • Contracts and pricing
  • Financial reports and forecasts
  • Unpublished plans and strategy
  • Source code and system details
  • Internal emails and reports

Usually fine

  • Public information
  • General questions
  • Your own writing, names removed
  • Made-up or sample data
  • Published articles and reports
  • Templates and outlines

Not sure which group? Treat it as the stricter one.

A sample lead workflow

From enquiry to qualification to a person who follows up

1. Enquiry

Someone fills in the website form.

2. Log

Added to the CRM or a sheet automatically.

3. AI summary

Summarizes the request and suggests a fit score.

4. Qualify

A simple rule routes it using your criteria.

Strong fit

A salesperson is told to call today.

Unclear fit

A draft asks one question for more detail.

Poor fit

A polite draft points to free resources.

5. Human follow-up

A person reviews the draft, edits it and replies or calls. Nothing is sent without their OK.

2

3

Choose the simplest tool

A built-in feature, an assistant or a two-step automation.

4

Pilot with review

Two to four weeks, a person checking every output.

5

Measure

Time, errors and customer response against the baseline.

6

Improve, expand or stop

Fix what failed, document it, name an owner.

Prompt engineering vs role engineering

From asking well every time to defining the job once

Prompt engineering

One request at a time

You write the full request, context and all

The AI answers that one request

Next time, you explain everything again

You remember the rules and check each answer

Best forOne-off questions, drafts and ideas

Role engineering

A teammate with a standing job

You define the role, mission and limits once

It keeps its sources, context and your preferences

It uses connected apps and can run on a schedule

It asks before anything risky, and you review

Best forWork that repeats every week

builds on

Role engineering doesn’t replace prompting. A good role instruction is still a well-written prompt, with its context, kept on file.

The Role Engineering Blueprint

Six parts that turn an AI assistant into a dependable teammate

1

Identity

What is its professional role?

Example: marketing strategist for a family-run coffee roaster.

2

Mission

What outcome is it responsible for?

Example: a monthly campaign plan that grows subscriptions.

3

Knowledge

What information does it need?

Example: brand guide, past campaigns, customer reviews.

4

Tools

What apps can it use?

Example: Google Drive and the web browser. No ad accounts yet.

5

Boundaries

What needs your approval?

Example: drafts freely; never publishes, sends or spends.

6

Performance

How will you judge its work?

Example: accurate claims, on-brand voice, usable with light edits.

The Bot works on its own

  • Researches and reads sources
  • Drafts emails, posts and reports
  • Organizes files and notes
  • Prepares summaries and options

It pauses and asks first

  • Sending or publishing anything
  • Buying or spending money
  • Deleting or overwriting records
  • Changing access or settings
